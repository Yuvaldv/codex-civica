# Computer misuse offences

## 1. Unauthorised access to computer material. <span id="section-1" />

### (1) <span id="section-1-1" />

A person is guilty of an offence if—

#### (a) <span id="section-1-1-a" />

he causes a computer to perform any function with intent to secure access to any program or data held in any computer [, or to enable any such access to be secured][^fc19758821];

#### (b) <span id="section-1-1-b" />

the access he intends to secure [, or to enable to be secured,][^fc19759931]  is unauthorised; and

#### (c) <span id="section-1-1-c" />

he knows at the time when he causes the computer to perform the function that that is the case.

### (2) <span id="section-1-2" />

The intent a person has to have to commit an offence under this section need not be directed at—

#### (a) <span id="section-1-2-a" />

any particular program or data;

#### (b) <span id="section-1-2-b" />

a program or data of any particular kind; or

#### (c) <span id="section-1-2-c" />

a program or data held in any particular computer.

### (3) <span id="section-1-3" />

[A person guilty of an offence under this section shall be liable—][^fc19760271]

#### (a) <span id="section-1-3-a" />

[on summary conviction in England and Wales, to imprisonment for a term not exceeding  ][^fc19760271][[the general limit in a magistrates’ court][^fkey-973120fe8be98c61aa292f3d2a639319]][^fc19760271][  or to a fine not exceeding the statutory maximum or to both;][^fc19760271]

#### (b) <span id="section-1-3-b" />

[on summary conviction in Scotland, to imprisonment for a term not exceeding ][^fc19760271][[12][^fkey-f4bd0915e8c2eb538a4f1fb4c1af5761]][^fc19760271][ months or to a fine not exceeding the statutory maximum or to both;][^fc19760271]

#### (c) <span id="section-1-3-c" />

[on conviction on indictment, to imprisonment for a term not exceeding two years or to a fine or to both.][^fc19760271]

## 2. Unauthorised access with intent to commit or facilitate commission of further offences. <span id="section-2" />

### (1) <span id="section-2-1" />

A person is guilty of an offence under this section if he commits an offence under section 1 above (“the unauthorised access offence”) with intent—

#### (a) <span id="section-2-1-a" />

to commit an offence to which this section applies; or

#### (b) <span id="section-2-1-b" />

to facilitate the commission of such an offence (whether by himself or by any other person);

and the offence he intends to commit or facilitate is referred to below in this section as the further offence.

### (2) <span id="section-2-2" />

This section applies to offences—

#### (a) <span id="section-2-2-a" />

for which the sentence is fixed by law; or

#### (b) <span id="section-2-2-b" />

for which a person who has attained the age of twenty-one years (eighteen in relation to England and Wales) and has no previous convictions may be sentenced to imprisonment for a term of five years (or, in England and Wales, might be so sentenced but for the restrictions imposed by section 33 of the [^fc8077941]Magistrates’ Courts Act 1980).

### (3) <span id="section-2-3" />

It is immaterial for the purposes of this section whether the further offence is to be committed on the same occasion as the unauthorised access offence or on any future occasion.

### (4) <span id="section-2-4" />

A person may be guilty of an offence under this section even though the facts are such that the commission of the further offence is impossible.

# Jurisdiction

## 4. Territorial scope of <span id="section-4" />

### (1) <span id="section-4-1" />

Except as provided below in this section, it is immaterial for the purposes of any offence under [section 1, 3 or 3ZA][^fkey-35a4e54ceb6d3707533671277fda5f47] above—

#### (a) <span id="section-4-1-a" />

whether any act or other event proof of which is required for conviction of the offence occurred in the home country concerned; or

#### (b) <span id="section-4-1-b" />

whether the accused was in the home country concerned at the time of any such act or event.

### (2) <span id="section-4-2" />

Subject to subsection (3) below, in the case of such an offence at least one significant link with domestic jurisdiction must exist in the circumstances of the case for the offence to be committed.

### (3) <span id="section-4-3" />

There is no need for any such link to exist for the commission of an offence under section 1 above to be established in proof of an allegation to that effect in proceedings for an offence under section 2 above.

### (4) <span id="section-4-4" />

Subject to section 8 below, where—

#### (a) <span id="section-4-4-a" />

any such link does in fact exist in the case of an offence under section 1 above; and

#### (b) <span id="section-4-4-b" />

commission of that offence is alleged in proceedings for an offence under section 2 above;

section 2 above shall apply as if anything the accused intended to do or facilitate in any place outside the home country concerned which would be an offence to which section 2 applies if it took place in the home country concerned were the offence in question.

### (4A) <span id="section-4-4A" />

### (5) <span id="section-4-5" />

This section is without prejudice to any jurisdiction exercisable by a court in Scotland apart from this section.

### (6) <span id="section-4-6" />

References in this Act to the home country concerned are references—

#### (a) <span id="section-4-6-a" />

in the application of this Act to England and Wales, to England and Wales;

## 17. Interpretation. <span id="section-17" />

### (1) <span id="section-17-1" />

The following provisions of this section apply for the interpretation of this Act.

### (2) <span id="section-17-2" />

A person secures access to any program or data held in a computer if by causing a computer to perform any function he—

#### (a) <span id="section-17-2-a" />

alters or erases the program or data;

#### (b) <span id="section-17-2-b" />

copies or moves it to any storage medium other than that in which it is held or to a different location in the storage medium in which it is held;

#### (c) <span id="section-17-2-c" />

uses it; or

#### (d) <span id="section-17-2-d" />

has it output from the computer in which it is held (whether by having it displayed or in any other manner);

and references to access to a program or data (and to an intent to secure such access  [or to enable such access to be secured][^fc19761061]) shall be read accordingly.

### (3) <span id="section-17-3" />

For the purposes of subsection (2)(c) above a person uses a program if the function he causes the computer to perform—

#### (a) <span id="section-17-3-a" />

causes the program to be executed; or

#### (b) <span id="section-17-3-b" />

is itself a function of the program.

### (4) <span id="section-17-4" />

For the purposes of subsection (2)(d) above—

#### (a) <span id="section-17-4-a" />

a program is output if the instructions of which it consists are output; and

#### (b) <span id="section-17-4-b" />

the form in which any such instructions or any other data is output (and in particular whether or not it represents a form in which, in the case of instructions, they are capable of being executed or, in the case of data, it is capable of being processed by a computer) is immaterial.

### (5) <span id="section-17-5" />

Access of any kind by any person to any program or data held in a computer is unauthorised if—

#### (a) <span id="section-17-5-a" />

he is not himself entitled to control access of the kind in question to the program or data; and

#### (b) <span id="section-17-5-b" />

he does not have consent to access by him of the kind in question to the program or data from any person who is so entitled [but this subsection is subject to section 10.][^fc8078421]

### (6) <span id="section-17-6" />

References to any program or data held in a computer include references to any program or data held in any removable storage medium which is for the time being in the computer; and a computer is to be regarded as containing any program or data held in any such medium.
