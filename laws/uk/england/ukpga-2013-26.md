Be it enacted by the Queen's most Excellent Majesty, by and with the advice and consent of the Lords Spiritual and Temporal, and Commons, in this present Parliament assembled, and by the authority of the same, as follows:—

# 

## 1. Serious harm <span id="section-1" />

### (1) <span id="section-1-1" />

A statement is not defamatory unless its publication has caused or is likely to cause serious harm to the reputation of the claimant.

### (2) <span id="section-1-2" />

For the purposes of this section, harm to the reputation of a body that trades for profit is not  “serious harm” unless it has caused or is likely to cause the body serious financial loss.

# 

## 2. Truth <span id="section-2" />

> [Extent — 2. Truth] E+W

### (1) <span id="section-2-1" />

It is a defence to an action for defamation for the defendant to show that the imputation conveyed by the statement complained of is substantially true.

### (2) <span id="section-2-2" />

Subsection (3) applies in an action for defamation if the statement complained of conveys two or more distinct imputations.

### (3) <span id="section-2-3" />

If one or more of the imputations is not shown to be substantially true, the defence under this section does not fail if, having regard to the imputations which are shown to be substantially true, the imputations which are not shown to be substantially true do not seriously harm the claimant's reputation.

### (4) <span id="section-2-4" />

The common law defence of justification is abolished and, accordingly, section 5 of the Defamation Act 1952 (justification) is repealed.

## 3. Honest opinion <span id="section-3" />

> [Extent — 3. Honest opinion] E+W

### (1) <span id="section-3-1" />

It is a defence to an action for defamation for the defendant to show that the following conditions are met.

### (2) <span id="section-3-2" />

The first condition is that the statement complained of was a statement of opinion.

### (3) <span id="section-3-3" />

The second condition is that the statement complained of indicated, whether in general or specific terms, the basis of the opinion.

### (4) <span id="section-3-4" />

The third condition is that an honest person could have held the opinion on the basis of—

#### (a) <span id="section-3-4-a" />

any fact which existed at the time the statement complained of was published;

#### (b) <span id="section-3-4-b" />

anything asserted to be a fact in a privileged statement published before the statement complained of.

### (5) <span id="section-3-5" />

The defence is defeated if the claimant shows that the defendant did not hold the opinion.

### (6) <span id="section-3-6" />

Subsection (5) does not apply in a case where the statement complained of was published by the defendant but made by another person (“the author”); and in such a case the defence is defeated if the claimant shows that the defendant knew or ought to have known that the author did not hold the opinion.

### (7) <span id="section-3-7" />

For the purposes of subsection (4)(b) a statement is a  “privileged statement” if the person responsible for its publication would have one or more of the following defences if an action for defamation were brought in respect of it—

#### (a) <span id="section-3-7-a" />

a defence under section 4 (publication on matter of public interest);

#### (b) <span id="section-3-7-b" />

a defence under section 6 (peer-reviewed statement in scientific or academic journal);

#### (c) <span id="section-3-7-c" />

a defence under section 14 of the Defamation Act 1996 (reports of court proceedings protected by absolute privilege);

#### (d) <span id="section-3-7-d" />

a defence under section 15 of that Act (other reports protected by qualified privilege).

### (8) <span id="section-3-8" />

The common law defence of fair comment is abolished and, accordingly, section 6 of the Defamation Act 1952 (fair comment) is repealed.

## 4. Publication on matter of public interest <span id="section-4" />

> [Extent — 4. Publication on matter of public interest] E+W

### (1) <span id="section-4-1" />

It is a defence to an action for defamation for the defendant to show that—

#### (a) <span id="section-4-1-a" />

the statement complained of was, or formed part of, a statement on a matter of public interest; and

#### (b) <span id="section-4-1-b" />

the defendant reasonably believed that publishing the statement complained of was in the public interest.

### (2) <span id="section-4-2" />

Subject to subsections (3) and (4), in determining whether the defendant has shown the matters mentioned in subsection (1), the court must have regard to all the circumstances of the case.

### (3) <span id="section-4-3" />

If the statement complained of was, or formed part of, an accurate and impartial account of a dispute to which the claimant was a party, the court must in determining whether it was reasonable for the defendant to believe that publishing the statement was in the public interest disregard any omission of the defendant to take steps to verify the truth of the imputation conveyed by it.

### (4) <span id="section-4-4" />

In determining whether it was reasonable for the defendant to believe that publishing the statement complained of was in the public interest, the court must make such allowance for editorial judgement as it considers appropriate.

### (5) <span id="section-4-5" />

For the avoidance of doubt, the defence under this section may be relied upon irrespective of whether the statement complained of is a statement of fact or a statement of opinion.

### (6) <span id="section-4-6" />

The common law defence known as the Reynolds defence is abolished.

## 5. Operators of websites <span id="section-5" />

> [Extent — 5. Operators of websites] E+W

### (1) <span id="section-5-1" />

This section applies where an action for defamation is brought against the operator of a website in respect of a statement posted on the website.

### (2) <span id="section-5-2" />

It is a defence for the operator to show that it was not the operator who posted the statement on the website.

### (3) <span id="section-5-3" />

The defence is defeated if the claimant shows that—

#### (a) <span id="section-5-3-a" />

it was not possible for the claimant to identify the person who posted the statement,

#### (b) <span id="section-5-3-b" />

the claimant gave the operator a notice of complaint in relation to the statement, and

#### (c) <span id="section-5-3-c" />

the operator failed to respond to the notice of complaint in accordance with any provision contained in regulations.

### (4) <span id="section-5-4" />

For the purposes of subsection (3)(a), it is possible for a claimant to  “identify” a person only if the claimant has sufficient information to bring proceedings against the person.

### (5) <span id="section-5-5" />

Regulations may—

#### (a) <span id="section-5-5-a" />

make provision as to the action required to be taken by an operator of a website in response to a notice of complaint (which may in particular include action relating to the identity or contact details of the person who posted the statement and action relating to its removal);

#### (b) <span id="section-5-5-b" />

make provision specifying a time limit for the taking of any such action;

#### (c) <span id="section-5-5-c" />

make provision conferring on the court a discretion to treat action taken after the expiry of a time limit as having been taken before the expiry;

#### (d) <span id="section-5-5-d" />

make any other provision for the purposes of this section.

### (6) <span id="section-5-6" />

Subject to any provision made by virtue of subsection (7), a notice of complaint is a notice which—

#### (a) <span id="section-5-6-a" />

specifies the complainant's name,

#### (b) <span id="section-5-6-b" />

sets out the statement concerned and explains why it is defamatory of the complainant,

#### (c) <span id="section-5-6-c" />

specifies where on the website the statement was posted, and

#### (d) <span id="section-5-6-d" />

contains such other information as may be specified in regulations.

### (7) <span id="section-5-7" />

Regulations may make provision about the circumstances in which a notice which is not a notice of complaint is to be treated as a notice of complaint for the purposes of this section or any provision made under it.

### (8) <span id="section-5-8" />

Regulations under this section—

#### (a) <span id="section-5-8-a" />

may make different provision for different circumstances;

#### (b) <span id="section-5-8-b" />

are to be made by statutory instrument.

### (9) <span id="section-5-9" />

A statutory instrument containing regulations under this section may not be made unless a draft of the instrument has been laid before, and approved by a resolution of, each House of Parliament.

### (10) <span id="section-5-10" />

In this section  “regulations” means regulations made by the Secretary of State.

### (11) <span id="section-5-11" />

The defence under this section is defeated if the claimant shows that the operator of the website has acted with malice in relation to the posting of the statement concerned.

### (12) <span id="section-5-12" />

The defence under this section is not defeated by reason only of the fact that the operator of the website moderates the statements posted on it by others.

## 8. Single publication rule <span id="section-8" />

### (1) <span id="section-8-1" />

This section applies if a person—

#### (a) <span id="section-8-1-a" />

publishes a statement to the public (“the first publication”), and

#### (b) <span id="section-8-1-b" />

subsequently publishes (whether or not to the public) that statement or a statement which is substantially the same.

### (2) <span id="section-8-2" />

In subsection (1)  “publication to the public” includes publication to a section of the public.

### (3) <span id="section-8-3" />

For the purposes of section 4A of the Limitation Act 1980 (time limit for actions for defamation etc) any cause of action against the person for defamation in respect of the subsequent publication is to be treated as having accrued on the date of the first publication.

### (4) <span id="section-8-4" />

This section does not apply in relation to the subsequent publication if the manner of that publication is materially different from the manner of the first publication.

### (5) <span id="section-8-5" />

In determining whether the manner of a subsequent publication is materially different from the manner of the first publication, the matters to which the court may have regard include (amongst other matters)—

#### (a) <span id="section-8-5-a" />

the level of prominence that a statement is given;

#### (b) <span id="section-8-5-b" />

the extent of the subsequent publication.

### (6) <span id="section-8-6" />

Where this section applies—

#### (a) <span id="section-8-6-a" />

it does not affect the court's discretion under section 32A of the Limitation Act 1980 (discretionary exclusion of time limit for actions for defamation etc), and

#### (b) <span id="section-8-6-b" />

the reference in subsection (1)(a) of that section to the operation of section 4A of that Act is a reference to the operation of section 4A together with this section.

## 10. Action against a person who was not the author, editor etc <span id="section-10" />

### (1) <span id="section-10-1" />

A court does not have jurisdiction to hear and determine an action for defamation brought against a person who was not the author, editor or publisher of the statement complained of unless the court is satisfied that it is not reasonably practicable for an action to be brought against the author, editor or publisher.

### (2) <span id="section-10-2" />

In this section  “author”,  “editor” and  “publisher” have the same meaning as in section 1 of the Defamation Act 1996.

## 12. Power of court to order a summary of its judgment to be published <span id="section-12" />

### (1) <span id="section-12-1" />

Where a court gives judgment for the claimant in an action for defamation the court may order the defendant to publish a summary of the judgment.

### (2) <span id="section-12-2" />

The wording of any summary and the time, manner, form and place of its publication are to be for the parties to agree.

### (3) <span id="section-12-3" />

If the parties cannot agree on the wording, the wording is to be settled by the court.

### (4) <span id="section-12-4" />

If the parties cannot agree on the time, manner, form or place of publication, the court may give such directions as to those matters as it considers reasonable and practicable in the circumstances.

### (5) <span id="section-12-5" />

This section does not apply where the court gives judgment for the claimant under section 8(3) of the Defamation Act 1996 (summary disposal of claims).
